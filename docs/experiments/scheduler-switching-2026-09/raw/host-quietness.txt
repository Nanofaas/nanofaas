# Was the host quiet while the campaign ran?

The campaign is a latency, CPU and post-GC heap measurement. A concurrent CPU-bound run does not
corrupt a file, it corrupts the number, silently — so this records what was checked and, just as
importantly, what was not.

## What was controlled

- **Build.** `run.sh` stops Gradle's daemon (`./gradlew --stop`) immediately before the measured JVM
  starts, so no Gradle process — daemon, worker or build — existed at any point during a run. The
  classpath was resolved and the harness compiled before that point.
- **Serialisation.** The smoke run, the campaign and the six window-length diagnostics were run one
  at a time, never concurrently, and nothing else CPU-bound was started while any of them ran. The
  campaign this file reports on began at `2026-09-21T14:07:15.862268874Z`.
- **Host.** gx10-1b52, 20 CPUs, Linux 6.17.0-1032-nvidia aarch64 (Linux, kernel
  Linux 6.17.0-1032-nvidia aarch64).

## Coverage limit — stated, not glossed

The sampler's 2026-09-21T15:38:06+02:00–2026-09-21T16:07:21+02:00 span covers the window-length diagnostics and the tail
of the preceding full run, and it ends within seconds of this campaign's own start. **The campaign
itself is not covered by that sampling.** For the campaign the evidence is the readings either side
of it, the absence of any Gradle process, and the fact that nothing else was started — plus the
indicator below, which is the one that comes from the campaign's own artifact.

## The in-artifact indicator

A host with a competing CPU-bound process does not produce repetitions that agree closely. In
`raw/full.jsonl` the useful-throughput spread across the five repetitions, over all 48
(workload, arm) pairs, has a **median of 3.12 %**, with 37 of the 48 under 5 % and 40 of the 48
under 10 %. That is the shape of an undisturbed host, and it is a property of the recorded artifact
rather than of anything observed outside it.

The wide end is explained by the workloads, not by the host: the four widest arms (31.9 %) are all
`low-load`, whose offered rate is 20/s, so a 2.5 s window holds about 50 arrivals and the count
itself varies. Those four arms report the *identical* range in every one of them — 208, 188, 174, 212 and 194
offerings in reps 1–5, the same count in all four arms. That is **not** a general property of this
harness, and this indicator therefore covers `low-load` only. Six groups of sixty in
`raw/steady.jsonl` have identical counts across all four arms, and five of the six are `low-load`'s;
the sixth is `unqueued` repetition 4. The other fifty-four differ, by up to 301 tickets
(`switch-under-load`, repetition 3: 10 680 against 10 981).

The spread scales with the offered rate, which is the useful fact: 0 tickets at 20/s (`low-load`),
0–15 at 300/s (`unqueued`), 4–81 at 3 540/s, 37–96 at 2 000/s, 87–301 at 1 040/s
(`switch-under-load`). Two mechanisms are consistent with that and this campaign does not separate
them: an extra offer — a retry or a refill — shifts every later draw in that arm, because one
Random drives the inter-arrival interval, the kind draw and the function choice; and the arrival
loop stops when the span closes, so whether the final arrival lands inside the span is a race that
is worth more tickets the more tickets there are. So the arms are compared under the same offered
*rate*, not over the same arrival *sequence*.

## What this does not establish

It does not certify the machine was idle. Other sessions on this host were alive throughout (an
editor, and other agent processes at a few percent of one core each). The claim this file supports
is the narrower one: no Gradle build, no second sampling run and no other CPU-bound work of the
kind that would move these figures was running, and the campaign's own dispersion is consistent
with that.

## The settlement campaign (round 3)

`/proc/loadavg` (1-minute) sampled every 45 s, 72 samples from 2026-09-21T16:54:03+02:00 to
2026-09-21T17:47:19+02:00, load average min 0.05, max 1.37 on 20 CPUs. This sampler was started with
the campaign rather than after it, so unlike the round-1 sampling it covers the run: the
completed-sample counter in the same file goes from 0 to 240 across it. The last few samples fall
after the campaign had finished (the counter already at 240) and are included rather than trimmed.
The samples themselves are committed beside this file, in `raw/load-average-samples-steady.txt`, so
these figures can be checked against the data rather than taken on trust. No Gradle daemon
existed during it (`run.sh` stops it before the measured JVM starts) and nothing else CPU-bound was
started.

The same in-artifact indicator applies to `raw/steady.jsonl`: 240 of 240 runs conserve work, none
reports a driver failure, and the useful-throughput spread across the five repetitions of each of
the 48 (workload, arm) pairs is the shape an undisturbed host produces.
